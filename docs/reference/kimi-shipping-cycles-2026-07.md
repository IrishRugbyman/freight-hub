## Thesis

No single historical period explains all three subsectors at once. The container orderbook
resembles 2007, dry bulk resembles the post-2016 period, and tankers are a new regime that
emerged in 2022. The answer must be segmented by vessel type.

- **Same case:** if the delivery wave coincides with the end of rerouting, container shipping
  enters a 2009-2016-style shakeout.
- **Different case:** the dry bulk and tanker orderbooks are thin, and their prosperity is not
  determined by the container clock.

## Section-by-section

**1. One industry, three clocks.** Cycle dials per subsector, positioned Top / early downturn /
profit pressure / clearing / bottom observation / early recovery / re-expansion.

**2. Nineteen years of the anchor company.** COSCO Shipping Holdings forward-adjusted monthly
price, 2007-06 to 2026-07, 225 months, nothing smoothed. Listed 2007-06-26 at RMB 8.48, opened
RMB 15.52, nominal peak RMB 68.4 in 2007-10 (forward-adjusted 23.57) two months before the BDI
collapse. 2011/2012 attributable net profit -10.449bn / -9.559bn RMB, renamed `*ST` on 2013-03-29.
2016-02 merger with China Shipping container shipping, dry bulk divested, 2016 full year -9.906bn;
BDI record low 290 on 2016-02-10. 2020-05 forward-adjusted low RMB 1.33 (19-year monthly low 1.14
in 2013-07). 2021/2022 attributable profit 89.296bn / 109.595bn RMB against the SCFI peak of
5,109.60 in 2022-01. Red Sea crisis from 2023-12, SCFI 3,733.8 on 2024-07-05. US/Israel strike on
Iran 2026-02-28, Hormuz closed in March, first simultaneous blockage of both corridors.

**3. Three curves since 1988.** SCFI (circles) and CCFI (squares) as disclosed points only, never
connected or interpolated; BDI monthly mean (454 obs); BDTI monthly mean (296 obs, nothing before
2001-12). Deliberately not spliced. Window notes: 1988-2002 shipping-conference pricing, no public
container index (CCFI launched 1998-04-13); 2003-2008 real WTO demand plus credit-financed 56-68%
orderbooks, BDI 11,793 to 663 in seven months; 2009-2016 eight years of clearing at BDI 290 and
CCFI 632.36; 2017-2019 fragile recovery, BDI 595-2,518; 2020-2022 one pandemic, three fates
(SCFI ~1,000 to 5,109.6 while tankers posted five consecutive loss-making quarters).

**4. The 2008 cohort.** 13 container lines, 8 dry bulk, 5 tanker owners; roughly half no longer at
the table. Hanjin: receivership 2016-08-31, declared bankrupt 2017-02-17, largest container
bankruptcy in 50+ years. DryShips repeated reverse splits; Genco and OSG through Chapter 11;
APL/NOL, OOCL and Hamburg Sud acquired; China Shipping Container Lines into COSCO; HMM on state
support; Eagle Bulk into Star Bulk; Golden Ocean and Euronav into CMB.TECH.

**5. Quarterly margins 2011-2016.** Annual reports' "two years of massive losses" broken into
quarters shows six years of persistently negative margins. The positive pulses in 2014-2015 were
asset-sale quarters; ex-non-recurring stayed negative. Quarterly values derived by differencing
cumulative statements.

**6. Orderbook piles 2007-2026.** Container 56% (2007) and dry bulk 67.6% (2008-07) were borrowed
prosperity. 2026: container 38.7%, dry bulk 7%, tanker 14.7%. Only container borrowed the leverage
back.

**7. Lead-lag.** Container rates lead earnings by 1-4 quarters (long-term contract buffering);
tanker rates by 1-2 quarters (spot). "People who read freight rates always arrive earlier than
people who read income statements."

**8. Five-window scorecard.** 1988-2002 negative control (no container index); 2003-2008 mixed
(demand pull plus orderbook leverage); 2009-2016 same script (oversupply, peak drawdown -43%);
2017-2019 different (policy, low orderbook); 2020-2022 event sample (orderbook 8.4% to 23.1%).
Two analogies explicitly rejected: the 1973-1986 tanker boom-bust (predates the window, no liner
market, non-splicable definitions) and "2021 dry bulk and container are one big cycle" (different
drivers). **Rule preserved: shape similarity is not mechanism identity.**

**9. Transmission bridge.** Crosses subsectors: orders and orderbook leverage, newbuild and
second-hand vessel prices, risk pricing and equity sentiment. Does not cross: cargo types and
demand drivers, contract structures, index definitions. Stated prohibitions: container orderbook
cannot be extrapolated to dry bulk; BDI does not substitute for container freight rates; tanker
earnings cannot be extrapolated to container profit.

**10. Watchlist.** Eight signals across price, capacity, event and financials, each with current
value, threshold, expected lag and falsifier. This is the section `/cycle` is modelled on - see
the table below.

**11. Gap register.** Reproduced under "Limitations the report discloses".

## Limitations the report discloses

- Continuous weekly CCFI/SCFI is not available machine-readable (SSE subscription); the container
  chart uses only official observations at key dates and never interpolates.
- 1988-2002 has no public unified container index; BDI is background only, not a substitute.
- iFinD monthly history starts ~2019; 2007-2018 for 601919 uses akshare (upstream sina,
  forward-adjusted), definitions kept in separate columns.
- yfinance `^BDIY` returns 404; BDI/BCI/BPI/BCTI/BDTI come from akshare. After Golden Ocean merged
  into CMB.TECH in 2025-08 Yahoo kept only 2 rows, so the dry-bulk peer set uses SBLK and 601872.
- The rebased cross-market chart is in local currency, no FX conversion.
- COSCO and CMES quarterly figures are derived by differencing cumulative statements; 2016 carries
  non-recurring restructuring items and 2014-2015 asset-sale spikes.
- China Merchants Energy Shipping is diversified (tanker/dry bulk/LNG/ro-ro), so its margin is not
  a pure-play tanker metric.
- Two generated-explainer slots were replaced with visible gaps because image generation was
  unavailable in that session.
- The Feb-Jul 2026 Hormuz/Red Sea situation is still evolving; figures are as of 2026-07-10.

## Corrections found on verification (2026-07-26)

Every figure the report supplied was checked against a primary or free trade-press source
before going on the site. Two of the four were materially wrong, and both in the same
direction - the report's supply-side bull case for dry bulk and tankers no longer holds:

| Report claim | Verified | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Dry-bulk orderbook ~7%, "multi-year low" (Clarksons via Benzinga, 2026-02-16) | 14% of fleet on a capacity basis (Clarksons H1-2026 review); 11.0% by vessel count, up from 9.5% a year earlier, orderbook +20% YoY against 3.8% fleet growth (Breakwave Advisors, 2026-07-07) | **Wrong.** Not a multi-year low and not stable - the ordering wave the report names as its own falsifier is under way |
| Tanker orderbook 14.7% (Riviera/Clarksons, 2025-11-10) | Crude tanker orderbook 130m dwt = 27% of the operational fleet; 151 VLCC contracts by mid-2026, largest tally since 1973 (BIMCO via IndexBox, 2026-07-09). Clarksons has all tankers at 25%; BIMCO had crude at 22% in Q1 | **Badly stale.** Every current read is above the 20% overshoot threshold |
| Container orderbook 38.3-39% (Alphaliner, 2026-06-24) | 1,592 ships / 12.98m TEU / 38.3% of the cellular fleet in service | **Confirmed** (we had recorded the top of the range; corrected to 38.3%) |
| SCFI 3,184.83 / CCFI 1,873.15 (SSE, 2026-07-10) | SSE publishes the latest two fixings free: SCFI 3,062.95 and CCFI 1,901.27, both 2026-07-24. The 07-10 pair could not be re-read (the page only shows two weeks) but the level is consistent | **Superseded** by a directly-read current fixing |

The lesson is the one the board is built around: a plausible, well-sourced-looking number in a
secondary summary is worth nothing until someone opens the primary. Two of these had drifted far
enough to invert the read on a subsector.

## What we took from it, and what we did not

Adopted: the three-clocks framing, the orderbook as the state variable that separates the
subsectors, the four-field signal contract (value / threshold / expected lag / falsifier), the
tiered-provenance and published-gap-register discipline, and the "shape similarity is not
mechanism identity" rule.

Not adopted: any of its numbers as data. None of the container series is machine-readable, the
whole thing is a single LLM research run, and the vs-2023 transit comparison is not computable
from our own AIS - collection began 2026-06-09. Anything quoted on the site is a `registered`
observation carrying its own primary source and as-of date. See `docs/ROADMAP_CYCLE.md`.
